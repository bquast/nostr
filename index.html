    Nostr is signed notes, passed around by simple servers
    
    
    Nostr ("Notes and Other Stuff Transmitted by Relays") is a protocol, not an app. Your account is a key pair. Everything you post is a small JSON object called an event, signed with that key. Relays are servers that store events and send them to anyone who asks. Clients do the rest: they sign, publish, ask, and check.
    
    
    Because every event carries its own signature, no relay has to be trusted. A relay can refuse you, lose your data or change a copy. Your followers will notice the change and can ask a different relay. The film above follows one note through all of this.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    The run
    
      
    Your browser makes a new private key, derives the public key, writes a kind 1 note, hashes it into an id and signs it. Your client sends it to your write relays, and it puts your relay list on an indexer. Three followers ask their own relays for your notes, check each signature, and use the outbox model when their relays do not have it. Two of them reply and react, and the relays push that back to you.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    What's real
    
      
      
        
    • The private key, public key, npub and nsec (NIP-19 bech32).
      • 
        
    • The event, its serialization, the SHA-256 id and the BIP-340 Schnorr signature. The followers' replies and reactions are signed with their own keys.
      • 
        
    • Every check and cross on screen is a real verify call on that exact data.
      • 
        
    • The wire messages use the exact NIP-01 formats: EVENT, OK, REQ, EOSE.
      • 
        
    • Kinds and tags follow NIP-01, NIP-10, NIP-25 and NIP-65.
      •

    What's drawn to illustrate
    
      
      
        
    • The curve is y² = x³ + 7 over integers mod 251, not mod the real 256-bit prime. The hops are a real double-and-add on that small curve.
      • 
        
    • Relays, phones and the hashing rings are props. A relay is a program on a server.
      • 
        
    • Messages are drawn as lights. Real ones are text frames on a WebSocket.
      • 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    What's simplified
    
      
      
        
    • The film runs in story order. In real life, followers keep their subscriptions open before you post, so your note arrives tens of milliseconds after a relay stores it.
      • 
        
    • Clients use more than the outbox model to find people: relay hints in links (nprofile, nevent), NIP-05 names and caches.
      • 
        
    • Relays also enforce sizes, rate limits, payment and authentication (NIP-42). Only the signature check is shown.
      • 
        
    • The failures in "What if…" are made up. The relays named are real.

    The messages
    
      
    
        
    MessageDirectionMeaning
    
        
          
    ["EVENT", event]client → relayPublish a signed event.
    
          
    ["REQ", sub_id, filter…]client → relayStart a subscription: send stored events that match, then new ones as they come.
    
          
    ["CLOSE", sub_id]client → relayStop a subscription.
    
          
    ["EVENT", sub_id, event]relay → clientAn event that matches your subscription.
    
          
    ["OK", id, true|false, msg]relay → clientThe result of a publish. A refusal has a reason prefix such as blocked: or invalid:.
    
          
    ["EOSE", sub_id]relay → clientEnd of stored events. Live events follow.
    
          
    ["CLOSED", sub_id, msg]relay → clientThe relay ended or refused a subscription.
    
          
    ["NOTICE", msg]relay → clientA human-readable message.
